Lawn Maintenance

Mowing, edging and trimming on a schedule you can set your watch by.

A good lawn is mostly cutting height and consistency. Taking off more than about a third of the blade at once stresses the plant and invites weeds, while cutting higher shades the soil, keeps roots cooler and crowds weeds out on its own. Sharp blades matter too -- a torn tip browns off and a cut one doesn't.

Signs you need this

  • Grass browning at the tips after each cut
  • Weeds spreading through thinning areas
  • Growth outrunning the time you have
  • Edges and borders getting away from you

What's included

  • Weekly & biweekly
  • Edging & trimming
  • Clippings hauled off

What to expect

A reliable schedule, cutting height set for the season and the grass rather than for speed, edges done, and clippings handled appropriately.

Common questions

Straight answers

My AC is running but the air isn't cold. What's wrong?

Common causes are a failed capacitor, a dirty coil choking airflow, or low refrigerant. Worth knowing: refrigerant is not consumed. If it's low, there's a leak, and adding more without finding the leak is a repair that fails again next season.

How often does it need servicing?

Once a year per system is the usual guidance -- cooling checked in spring, heating in autumn. Most of the value is airflow and cleaning: a blocked coil or a collapsed filter makes equipment work harder and fail sooner.

Should I repair or replace an old system?

Age, the cost of the repair and what it's costing to run all matter. A cheap fix on an eight-year-old system is usually worth it. An expensive one on a system near the end of its life often isn't.

Is a bigger unit better?

No -- and this is the most common mistake in the trade. An oversized system cools quickly, shuts off, and never runs long enough to pull humidity out, so the house feels clammy and the equipment short-cycles itself to death. Correct sizing comes from the house's actual load.

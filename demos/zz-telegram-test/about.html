Electrical work is judged entirely on the parts you cannot see. A finished outlet looks the same whether the connection behind it is right or is the kind that heats up quietly inside a wall for a few years.

So the standard has to be the same whether or not anyone will ever look: fault traced rather than guessed, work done to code including the parts hidden by drywall, and the panel left labelled so the next person can work on it safely.

We cover Omaha.

How we work

The part most people care about

Traced, not guessed

A tripping breaker is a symptom. We find what's drawing the current rather than fitting a bigger breaker over the top of it.

Done to code

Including the parts nobody would see. Cutting a corner inside a wall is the kind that burns.

Labelled and documented

The panel gets marked up properly, so the next person -- including future you -- can find the right breaker.

How it works

What happens when you call

1

You call

Describe the symptom -- a breaker that keeps tripping, lights that dim when the AC starts, outlets that stopped working, or a project you're planning.

2

We trace it

Electrical faults hide. We test the circuit rather than replacing parts until the symptom goes away, because a breaker that trips is usually doing its job.

3

You get a clear scope

What needs doing, what's code-required alongside it, and what it costs -- in writing, before anything is opened up.

4

We do it safely and tidily

Work done to code, circuits labelled properly, and permits pulled where the work calls for them.
